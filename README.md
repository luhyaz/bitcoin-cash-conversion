## Three ways to turn BTC into spendable money

### 1. Express Sell

Express Sell is designed for users who want a quoted sale without placing a traditional order-book trade.

You select:

- Bitcoin as the crypto
- The fiat currency you want
- The amount to sell
- An available payment method

The platform then shows a quote before you confirm. The quote can include the exchange rate, payout amount, limits, and any applicable spread or service charge.

This route is practical when the goal is speed and simplicity. It is less useful if you want to control the exact BTC selling price or compare multiple market orders. You are accepting the quote shown by the payment route rather than setting a limit order on the order book.

For a small or moderate cash-out, Express Sell may be the least confusing option. For a large amount, compare the preview carefully because a convenient quote can still include a spread.

### 2. Spot trading

Spot trading gives you more control over the sale price.

You can sell BTC against an available trading pair, such as BTC/USDT or another supported market. Depending on the pair and your region, you may then need to convert the proceeds into a cash balance or withdraw another asset.

A spot sale involves an order-book fee. OKX separates **maker** and **taker** fees:

- A **maker** order adds liquidity to the order book, usually through a limit order that is not filled immediately.
- A **taker** order removes liquidity by matching an existing order, commonly through a market order or immediately executable limit order.

The current OKX fee schedule lists a Regular user rate of **0.2000% maker** and **0.3500% taker** for standard spot trading, while higher VIP tiers have lower rates. Fee tiers are based on assets under management or 30-day trading volume and are updated daily.

Spot trading can be preferable when:

- You want to choose a limit price.
- You are selling a larger amount.
- You want to compare the order-book price with an instant quote.
- You understand trading pairs and order types.

It adds more decisions, though. You need to select the right market, check liquidity, choose the order type, and verify the amount received after fees.

### 3. P2P selling

P2P means peer-to-peer. Instead of selling directly through an exchange quote, you sell Bitcoin to another user who pays you through an available payment method.

OKX’s P2P instructions say the platform holds the crypto while the buyer pays. The seller should confirm that the money has actually arrived in the bank or payment account before releasing the crypto. OKX also warns users not to rely only on SMS notifications or screenshots as proof of payment.

A typical P2P sale works like this:

1. Open **P2P Trading**.
2. Choose **Sell**.
3. Select Bitcoin and the fiat currency you want.
4. Filter offers by payment method, amount, and other available criteria.
5. Select an offer.
6. Enter the amount of BTC to sell.
7. Wait for the buyer to send payment.
8. Check your actual bank or payment account.
9. Release the Bitcoin only after the payment is confirmed.

P2P can be useful when direct fiat payout options are limited. It also requires more attention than Express Sell. A buyer’s payment can be delayed, reversed, or sent from an account that does not match the trade details. Follow the order instructions exactly and do not release BTC because someone sends a message saying that payment is complete.

## OKX fees when converting Bitcoin to cash

The word “fee” can refer to several different costs. Treating them as one number is a reliable way to miscalculate your final payout.

### Trading fee

If you sell BTC through the spot order book, OKX applies the maker or taker fee for your account tier and trading pair.

For a Regular user on the current standard fee schedule:

- Maker fee: **0.2000%**
- Taker fee: **0.3500%**

For example, a 0.3500% taker fee on a $1,000 spot sale would be approximately $3.50 before considering the spread, network fees, or any separate withdrawal charge. The actual fee is calculated using the filled amount and the applicable fee rate.

### Spread

An instant Sell or Convert quote may include a spread between the reference market price and the price offered for the transaction. A screen can show “no transaction fee” while the quoted exchange rate is still different from the mid-market price.

OKX’s own help material distinguishes between Sell, Convert, and spot trading. Sell uses a quote, Convert exchanges one eligible asset for another using a quote, and spot trading uses the order book.

The practical rule is simple: do not compare only the line labeled “fee.” Compare the final amount you receive with the amount you would expect from the market price.

### Withdrawal fee

If you sell BTC and then send crypto to another wallet or exchange, a withdrawal fee may apply. OKX states that deposits do not carry a network fee for the depositor, while crypto withdrawals are charged to the withdrawing party. The actual withdrawal amount can vary by asset and network.

For an on-chain BTC withdrawal, check:

- The selected network
- Minimum withdrawal amount
- Network fee
- Estimated processing time
- Destination address
- Whether the receiving platform supports that exact network

A Bitcoin address and a network selection are not interchangeable details. Sending through the wrong network can result in a loss of funds.

### Bank or payment-provider fees

Your bank, card provider, payment app, or third-party payment processor may charge its own fee. These costs are separate from OKX trading fees.

The final cash amount can therefore be affected by:

- The BTC market price
- The buy or sell spread
- Trading commission
- Payment-provider charges
- Bank conversion fees
- Withdrawal fees
- Tax withholding or reporting obligations in your jurisdiction

Before confirming a sale, check the net amount rather than focusing on a single fee percentage.

## A careful checklist before selling BTC

Use this short checklist before confirming the transaction:

1. **Confirm the amount.** Check whether you entered BTC or fiat. A decimal mistake is expensive.
2. **Check the destination.** Make sure the money is going to a cash balance, bank, card, payment app, or P2P account as intended.
3. **Review the quote.** Compare the displayed payout with the current market price.
4. **Check fees and spread.** “No transaction fee” does not automatically mean the quote has no spread.
5. **Verify limits.** Look at the minimum, maximum, and remaining daily limit.
6. **Use the correct network.** This applies whenever you withdraw crypto instead of fiat.
7. **Keep records.** Save the transaction details, sale price, fee, and date for your own accounting.
8. **Check local tax rules.** Selling Bitcoin may create a taxable disposal depending on your jurisdiction.
